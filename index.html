Trash Talk, Annotated

The Storylines

The Bridesmaid

Joe's long walk back to the podium

Joe won the league's very first title in 2007 as TD King 21 — a founding-father championship. Then came fourteen years of nearly-but-not-quite, and the ending got crueler the closer he got: in 2019 his Thielen my Chubb posted the league's best regular-season record (10-3) and still lost the final to Fat Ewoks. In 2020 he made the final again — and lost again, to Dream Team2. Two straight championship-game defeats after the best team in football all season. He finally broke through in 2021, blowing out Woolwich Football Team 153.00-80.40 in the final. Fourteen years between rings, with two gut-punch finals losses in the two seasons right before it — the longest and hardest title drought in league history.

The Dynasty (and the Crash)

Scott, Chemical Toilet, and the boom-bust-boom decade

Scott has been in this league since year one — his 2007 team was Banana Hammocks, two rebrands before Chemical Toilet even existed. Once it did, it became the league's closest thing to a dynasty: titles in 2010, 2013, and 2015. But sandwiched right in the middle of that run was 2014, when Chemical Toilet crashed to 1-12 — one year after a championship — before rebuilding to 10-3 and another ring the very next season. Then in 2021, it happened again, worse: a 1-13 season, one win off the all-time floor. For 2022 the Chemical Toilet name and roster passed to a manager named Drew, while Scott started over from scratch under a new name, I Like Turtles. He made the final in his first year back (2023, runner-up) and won it all in 2024. Two of the three worst seasons in league history belong to Scott's teams — and so do four of its championships.

The Streak

Rich's Dream Team2 run

Rich joined the league in 2017 and won it all immediately, then went back-to-back in 2018 — the only true repeat champion in league history besides Bill. Gati's Fat Ewoks broke the streak in 2019, but Rich took the title right back in 2020: three rings in four years from a first-year manager. Adding a fun family wrinkle: Rich is Bob's dad, meaning a Snozzberries championship in 2008/2012 and a Dream Team2 dynasty a decade later both run through the same family tree. Rich rebranded to Eagles Rock 2 in 2024.

The Nailbiter

The closest championship game in league history

2011: Phil's TiggleBitties beat Joe's Krumb Lord 137.40 to 136.15 — a margin of 1.25 points, the tightest title game the league has ever seen. It's Phil's first of two rings; the second came five years later, in 2016, when TiggleBitties beat Sex Panther by a much more comfortable 9 points.

The Comeback

Chris arrives, never rebrands

Every other manager in this league has cycled through team names — some of them a dozen times. Chris joined in 2017 as Cheesesteak's and has never once changed it. He also owns one of the wildest turnarounds on record: a 1-12 season in 2020, followed five years later by the 2025 championship — the longest doormat-to-champion gap in league history, all under the same unchanged name.

The Turnover

Who's actually been here since the start

The league likes to say it's the same faces since 2007, but the roster has churned more than the highlight reel suggests. Five original 2007-08 managers are gone: Rosetti (I'm a Beast!/BEAST/Dixie Normous) left after 2012, Charles Harkins (Murse) after 2013, daniel (The Mighty Wee-Men/Wee Gurley Man) after 2016, Jesse (Your Stepfather) after 2016, and Phil (TiggleBitties, a two-time champion) after 2022. Harry — one championship, 2014 — and Premal Colaco left after 2017, the same year Rich and Chris arrived. Sean joined in 2018 as Virginia Eagles and is still here; Jimmy ran Shake N Bake from 2019-2021; John and Drew both joined in 2022, backfilling Phil's slot and the Chemical Toilet name after Scott stepped away for the year. Of the ten managers in the league today, only four — Joe, Bob, Gati, and Bill — have been here for every single one of the nineteen seasons. Scott's been in the league since 2007 too, but sat out 2022 before rebuilding as I Like Turtles.

Roll Call

The Founding Cast

Four managers have been here for all nineteen seasons. The rest of the roster — Scott included — has turned over more than the trophy has.

Joe

League's first-ever champion (2007, TD King 21). Spent 14 years as the league's best bridesmaid — including back-to-back finals losses in 2019 and 2020 — before ring #2 in 2021. All 19 seasons, unbroken.

Bob

The Snozzberries, since day one. Two titles (2008, 2012). The league's most consistent contender — 17 playoff appearances in 19 seasons. Rich's son. All 19 seasons, unbroken.

Gati (DavidM)

Harry Plopper, Sex Panther, Fat Ewoks, Team Browhole, Woolwich Fighting Chickens — a rotating cast of alter egos, and two titles (2009, 2019) to show for it. All 19 seasons, unbroken.

Scott

Chemical Toilet dynasty architect turned crash-and-rebuild story: titles in 2010, 2013, 2015 and 2024, a 1-12 season in 2014 and a 1-13 season in 2021 — two of the three worst records in league history. Sat out 2022 after that 1-13 crash, handing the Chemical Toilet name to Drew, then came back in 2023 as I Like Turtles. Four rings, the most of anyone.

Bill (Max Bet)

Several early-era alter egos (Plow King, Raw Plower, Bearack Obearma). Back-to-back champion, 2022-2023 — the only other repeat champ besides Rich. League commissioner. All 19 seasons, unbroken.

Phil — departed after 2022

TiggleBitties, in the league since 2008. Two titles (2011 — the closest championship game ever, by 1.25 points — and 2016). Left the league after fifteen seasons.

Harry — departed after 2017

Harry Ballz. One title, 2014 — sandwiched between two of Scott's championships. In the league through at least 2013; left after 2017.

Rich

Joined 2017 and won the title in his first two seasons (2017, 2018) — an immediate back-to-back. Added a third in 2020 as Dream Team2. Rebranded to Eagles Rock 2 in 2024. Bob's dad.

Chris

Joined 2017 as Cheesesteak's and never changed the name once — through a 1-12 season in 2020 and a championship five years later, in 2025.

Sean — joined 2018

Virginia Eagles, unchanged since he joined. Steady mid-pack contender, never missed more than a couple playoffs running.

Drew & John — joined 2022

The league's newest core members. Drew took over the Chemical Toilet name and roster from Scott in 2022, rebranding to Stu Pedasso in 2024. John's Hawk Time backfilled a vacated slot the same year and made the final in its 2022 debut season.

The Rulebook

Keeper League Rules

Straight from the league's official rules doc. Money and league-officer details are historical (2016) and may be out of date — the football rules below still govern play.

Keeper Basics

You may keep 3 players per season. Each player can be held for a maximum of 2 years. Only players on your final roster from the previous season are keeper-eligible.

Keeper Cost

Keeping a player costs a draft pick one round earlier than the round they were drafted. A 1st-round pick can never be kept. Example: a 3rd-round pick costs your 2nd-round pick to keep.

2nd Year Keepers

Holding a player for a second season costs another round earlier than what it cost the first time. Example: an original 4th-round pick, kept once already at 3rd round, costs your 2nd-round pick to keep again.

Undrafted / Traded Keepers

An undrafted (waiver-acquired) player held as a keeper costs a flat 6th-round pick. A player acquired by trade, or drafted-and-dropped then picked up elsewhere, costs a pick in the round prior to whatever round they were originally drafted in. You can't trade for a pick after the season ends just to make a keeper cost work.

League Officers & Fees

The Business Side

Officers (2016): Commissioner/Treasurer Bill Smitheman · League Secretary Dave "Gati" Morgado · Trade Committee: Bill Smitheman, Dave Morgado, Joe Segrist, alternate Bob Baitzel.

Fees: $100 entry fee; $3 per player traded or dropped/added (e.g. a 2-player trade between 2 teams costs each team $3). Fines are $5 per infraction.

Prize fund: 1st place — $550 + 25% of transaction fees. 2nd place — $250. 3rd place — $150. Consolation bracket winner — $50 plus first choice of draft slot (picks 1–4). Transaction fees also fund the league's high-game and season-high-points bonuses (25% / 50% splits).

New rules: Voted on by online survey each year, run by the league secretary. Proposed rule changes go to the secretary or commissioner before the survey is created, and take effect the same season.

Trading

Players & Draft Picks

Trading Players

Propose a trade from your team page; the other manager can accept, decline, or counter. Both managers must consent — an unanswered offer auto-cancels after 10 days. Accepted trades are reviewed by the commissioner (veto window per league config) before processing. A trade is voided if any involved player has moved off the team they were on when the deal was accepted. No manager may have more than 5 pending trades at once. Trade deadline is 11:59pm PT every Wednesday, reviewed by the Trade Committee (Commissioner, Dave Morgado, Joe Segrist, plus Bob Baitzel as alternate when a committee member is involved) by majority vote — a unanimous vote is required if two committee members are part of the trade. Last day for trades is after Week 10.

Trading Draft Picks

Allowed during the draft and league year only. Trading away a Round 1–2 pick requires pre-paying the full next-season league fee; Rounds 3–5 require half. That prepayment carries forward to next season (or offsets an incoming manager's fee if the original manager doesn't return).

Free Agency

Waivers & Free Agents

Waiver priority starts as the reverse of the draft order, then rolls: every successful claim sends that team to the back of the list. Un-owned players who've cleared waivers are free agents, available first-come-first-served. Dropped players go on waivers immediately and can't be reclaimed by the dropping team until the waiver period expires.

Game-Time Waivers

Each free agent hits waivers as soon as their game starts that week. Claims process at 11:59pm ET Tuesday. Bye-week players hit waivers at 8:30pm ET Monday. Bench players can be dropped any time, even mid-game — but active/starting players can't be touched within 5 minutes of their game's kickoff.

In-Season

Roster Notes

Every roster position must be filled — an empty starting spot is a $5 fine. You may claim-then-waive the same player at most twice in 24 hours. Bench players score no points. Future lineups you've pre-set will be wiped if you make any roster move (add/drop/trade) before then.

Postseason

Tiebreakers, Playoffs & Draft Order

Tiebreakers

Regular-season ties break by: overall win %, total points, then points scored in each week from Week 17 backward to Week 1, then a coin flip. Playoff-game ties break by: total starting-roster touchdowns, then QB points, then DEF points, then WR points, then RB points, then TE points, then K points, then a coin flip.

Playoffs — Top 6 Teams

Week 14: #1 seed bye, #4 vs #5, #3 vs #6, #2 seed bye. Week 15: #1 vs winner of (4/5), #2 vs winner of (3/6); losers of those two play for 5th place. Week 16: winners play the Championship; losers play for 3rd place.

Next Season's Draft Order

Reverse of this season's standings for playoff teams (the 5th-place slot goes to the winner of the 5th/6th place game). Non-playoff teams choose their draft slot from picks 1–4, in order starting with the worst finisher — each team's choice must be made a week before the draft, or they forfeit the choice and drop to last priority.

The Fine Print

Disputes & Commissioner Control

Disputes are decided by Trade Committee majority vote — file a complaint with the commissioner within 3 days of the disputed event; the committee has 2 days to rule. Anything not covered by these rules is also decided by committee vote. The commissioner can edit any roster or result at any time; joining the league means accepting that.

Other League Rules

Dave "Gati" Morgado is required to wear the small MJD jersey during each draft.
